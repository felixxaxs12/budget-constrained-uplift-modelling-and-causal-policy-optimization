# Prior art and contribution boundary

This is a targeted prior-art review, not a systematic review. Searches and source checks were completed on 2026-08-03 using publisher pages, proceedings, official dataset documentation, and primary papers.

## Verified evidence matrix

| Work | Data and design | Overlap | Boundary for this project |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Diemert et al. (2018), *A Large Scale Benchmark for Uplift Modeling*](https://www.adkdd.org/papers/a-large-scale-benchmark-for-uplift-modeling/2018) | Historical 25.3M-row Criteo v1 release from randomized incrementality tests. | Introduced the benchmark, sanity checks, and uplift ranking metrics. | It is not the primary source for the corrected 13.98M-row artifact and does not provide held-out DR capacity-frontier evaluation. |
| [Diemert et al. (2021), *A Large Scale Benchmark for Individual Treatment Effect Prediction and Uplift Modeling*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10106) | `CRITEO-UPLIFTv2`, 13,979,592 rows, 12 covariates, treatment ratio 0.85, and a correction for the v1 advertiser leak. | This is the primary data and design reference. It studies uplift and ITE metrics and baselines. | The present work adopts the data and does not claim a new dataset. It adds a locked equal-capacity comparison based on held-out DR policy value and paired uncertainty. |
| [Goldenberg et al. (2020), *Free Lunch! Retrospective Uplift Modeling for Dynamic Promotions Recommendation within ROI Constraints*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06293) | Booking.com randomized promotion experiments with dynamic treatment choice under return-on-investment constraints. | It is a foundational constrained-uplift application that links incremental effects to business allocation. | The present project studies one binary treatment and equal per-user capacity. It has neither observed monetary costs nor an ROI constraint, so it does not claim monetary budget optimization. |
| [Fernández-Loría and Provost (2022), *Causal Classification: Treatment Effect Estimation vs. Outcome Prediction*](https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/19-480.html) | Formal and empirical comparison of outcome prediction and treatment-effect estimation for causal classification, including advertising allocation. | It directly motivates comparing response ranking with treatment-effect ranking when only a top fraction can be treated. | The conceptual comparison is not new here. This project supplies an open, pre-specified Criteo v2 replication with held-out DR policy evaluation. |
| [Dudík, Langford, and Li (2011), *Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601) | Logged contextual-bandit policy evaluation. | Provides the theoretical basis for doubly robust off-policy value estimation. | It is adopted for evaluation and is not the source of the DR CATE learner. |
| [Kennedy (2023), *Towards Optimal Doubly Robust Estimation of Heterogeneous Causal Effects*](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-EJS2157) | Two-stage doubly robust pseudo-outcome estimation for heterogeneous effects. | Directly supports the cross-fitted DR learner used for ranking. | The estimator is adopted without a methodological novelty claim. |
| [Rößler and Schoder (2022), *Bridging the Gap*](https://doi.org/10.1177/10949968221111083) | Fifteen methods across 27 synthetic and six real datasets, including a Criteo release. | Broad uplift and HTE benchmarking with targeting metrics. | This project deliberately uses fewer models and emphasizes paired held-out decision value. The paper does not clearly name its Criteo release, so this project does not label it v1 or v2 as fact. |
| [Liu et al. (2024), *Benchmarking for Deep Uplift Modeling in Online Marketing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00335) | Thirteen deep uplift models on Criteo and Lazada with ranking metrics. | Provides a large reproducible model benchmark. | It does not make DR policy value the primary criterion. Its breadth supports keeping this project to a minimum sufficient model set rather than adding a deep-model tournament. |
| [Zhang et al. (2025), *Bi-Level Decision-Focused Causal Learning for Large-Scale Marketing Optimization*](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/93427bc08aef8f9d6d5366d70284f571-Abstract-Conference.html) | NeurIPS study using a hybrid observational/RCT construction from Criteo v2, with visit treated as cost and conversion as value. | Directly studies Criteo, budgeted decisions, and decision-focused causal learning. | This project retains the original randomized assignment, does not synthesize observational data or costs, and does not propose a new optimization algorithm. |
| [Jiang et al. (2026), *A Decision-Oriented Empirical Comparison of Predictive and Uplift-Based Scoring under Budget Constraints*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10791-026-10251-5) | Confidential observational digital-lending data, top-`k` policies, DR/AIPW value, and bootstrap analysis. | Predictive versus uplift scoring, common budgets, policy value, and ranking-versus-decision comparisons closely overlap. | The overall decision framework is not novel here. The defensible difference is an open, protocol-locked analysis of the official randomized artifact, without synthetic outcomes, conversion to an observational design, or generated costs, with a public manifest and paired uncertainty at each capacity. |
| [Pillai (2026), *Budget-Constrained Causal Bandits*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26169) | Preprint using a Criteo subsample, generated user costs, and online budget pacing. | Studies uplift-guided allocation under budget constraints. | This project is an offline full-artifact benchmark with equal-cost capacity and no generated costs, online exploration, or pacing. |

## Contribution after the search

Prior work already covers predictive-versus-uplift comparison, top-`k` targeting, DR/AIPW policy value, bootstrap uncertainty, and the distinction between ranking metrics and decision value. This project will not use "first" or claim a new estimator, optimizer, or decision framework.

The defensible contribution is an open, protocol-locked empirical replication using the official randomized `CRITEO-UPLIFTv2` `v2.1` artifact without synthetic outcomes, conversion to an observational design, or generated costs. It compares a deliberately small set of predictive and causal scores under identical equal-cost capacities, evaluates incremental conversion value on held-out rows using doubly robust scores, and reports paired uncertainty across the pre-specified capacity frontier. Treatment assignment, post-assignment exposure, offline-evaluated policy value, and realized business impact remain separate concepts.
